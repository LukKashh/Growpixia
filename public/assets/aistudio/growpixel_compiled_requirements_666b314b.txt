GROWPIXEL – COMPILED IMPLEMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

1.  BLOCK BREAKING DURATION & ANIMATION

-   Block breaking is currently almost instantaneous.
-   Restore a longer breaking duration, similar to the previous
    implementation.
-   Display breaking animations throughout the entire breaking process.
-   The player must hold the interaction on a block until breaking is
    completed.

2.  BLOCK PLACEMENT – PLAYER POSITION RESET

-   Placing a block currently appears to trigger a world rejoin.
-   The player is teleported back to the world spawn point.
-   Block placement must not trigger a world rejoin, respawn, or
    position reset.
-   Preserve the player’s current position and world state after
    placement, apart from the actual block being placed.

3.  SPAWN POINT – GROUND-LEVEL POSITIONING & APPEARANCE

-   The spawn point must be level with the ground and aligned with the
    ground tile.
-   Its visual representation should resemble a door.
-   It should clearly identify the world entrance and designated spawn
    location.

4.  SPAWN POINT – DEDICATED SYSTEM-GENERATED DOOR BLOCK

-   The spawn point must be implemented as a dedicated, system-generated
    block type, not an ordinary player-placed block.
-   Generate it automatically when a new world is created.
-   It functions as a background block.
-   Players can pass through it without collision obstruction.
-   When standing on it, a player can punch it to activate the
    leave-world functionality.
-   Its location is predetermined during world generation.
-   Its location is permanently locked.
-   No player, regardless of permissions, can break, remove, or replace
    it.
-   Its protection must function identically to Bedrock.

5.  FULL-SCREEN TOGGLE – MENU OPTION

-   Add a full-screen toggle option within the in-game menu.
-   Allow players to switch between full-screen and normal display modes
    through this menu option.
-   Make the option readily accessible without requiring external
    configuration.

6.  TOOLBAR – AUTOMATIC EMPTY SLOT RESTORATION

-   Toolbar slots currently retain item references/icons after those
    items have been consumed or are no longer in the player’s
    possession.
-   Automatically clear a toolbar slot when its item’s quantity reaches
    zero or the player no longer possesses that item.
-   Return the slot to its default empty appearance.
-   Keep the toolbar synchronized with the player’s actual inventory.
-   Do not leave outdated item icons or references in empty slots.

7.  TOOLBAR – FIXED THREE-SLOT RECENT-ITEM SYSTEM

-   The toolbar must have a fixed capacity of exactly three item slots.
-   Adding or collecting more items must never increase the number of
    toolbar slots.
-   Display the three most recently selected or collected items.
-   The toolbar is a quick-access system for recently selected/collected
    items, not an extension of the inventory.
-   The inventory remains the complete storage system.
-   When an item is depleted or is no longer in the player’s possession,
    its toolbar slot must become empty, as described in Requirement 6.

8.  FIST – SEPARATE TOOLBAR CONTROL

-   The Fist must not occupy one of the three toolbar slots.
-   Display it as a separate control immediately beside the toolbar row.
-   Keep it independently accessible regardless of which items occupy
    the toolbar.

9.  TOOLBAR & INVENTORY – ITEM TEXTURE REPRESENTATION

-   Remove numerical slot labels or numbering.
-   Use the exact texture of the corresponding item or block.
-   Use the same world-placement texture used to represent that
    item/block in the game world.
-   Scale the texture down to fit the available slot diameter.
-   Apply this visual representation consistently in both the inventory
    and toolbar.
-   Preserve the original texture’s appearance when resizing; do not
    substitute generic icons or alternative artwork.

10. LOCK PLACEMENT – RESTRICTED AREA & OVERLAP HANDLING

-   If the intended lock placement location is already inside an area
    protected by an existing lock, do not allow another lock to be
    placed there.
-   Two locks must never independently claim the same world tiles.
-   Allow a new lock to be placed near an existing lock when its
    resulting claim can fit without overlapping.
-   If the new lock’s originally calculated claim area would overlap an
    existing protected area, cut off the overlapping portion of the new
    claim.
-   The new claim boundary must end at the existing claim’s border,
    allowing adjacent territories with shared borders but no overlap.
-   Placing a new lock must never reduce, modify, or overwrite the area
    belonging to an existing lock.

11. LOCKS – DESTRUCTION PERMISSIONS & INVENTORY RETURN

-   Locks must be breakable; they must not be completely indestructible.
-   Only the direct/original player who placed a lock may break, remove,
    or destroy it.
-   Verify the original placing player’s identity before allowing
    destruction.
-   No other player may destroy that lock, regardless of their
    permissions elsewhere.
-   When the original owner destroys their lock, return the lock
    immediately to that same player’s inventory.
-   The lock must be restored as an available inventory item, not
    permanently consumed or lost.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

-   The Spawn Door is a system-generated world object, not an ordinary
    decorative door texture or player-placed block. It is permanently
    protected like Bedrock.
-   Player-placed locks are different: they are protected against
    unauthorized destruction, but are not indestructible. Only their
    original placing player can destroy them, and the destroyed lock
    returns to that player’s inventory.
-   Lock claims may be adjacent, but must never overlap. An existing
    claim always retains its full protected area.
-   The toolbar is limited to three recent-item shortcuts; it is not the
    player’s inventory.
